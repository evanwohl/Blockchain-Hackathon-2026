# FreightOS judge walkthrough

**One-minute driver walkthrough.** Start on Overview and open **Price my fuel stop**. The customer offers $90 now for a $3 fee. The driver brings $75 and needs $25 from the wallet. Toggle **No new loan** to see $2.06 left on payday, below the $3 target. Toggle **Borrowing allowed** to see how a $4.57 loan leaves $6.92 at the $150 SOL stress. Open the lower-price comparison to show the tradeoff, then save the driver's funding sheet. On the same screen, **Inspect a settled loan on Devnet** has both signed transactions from a separate $7 example. The $190 plan is a recorded mainnet estimate.


**Show when borrowing earns its keep.** Under **Your funding terms**, enter a $745 stop, $3,100 invoice, $250 early for $5, $250 cash, $600 of SOL, and $150 spendable wallet dollars. Open the payday price comparison. The last $95 can come from selling SOL or borrowing against it. Selling leaves 40¢ more at the recorded opening price. Borrowing leaves more from $72.79/SOL and adds $101.69 at $150/SOL if the pledge survives until payday. Point to the $95.83 payoff and the 2.8534 SOL locked. This is a conditional funding decision. The separate Devnet receipt shows execution.

**Show why combining DeFi products matters.** On the default funding desk, choose **Compare keeping $4.18 more in SOL today**. FreightOS borrows $0.91, sells enough SOL for $3.66 and still leaves the requested $3 on payday in the highest tested price scenario. For the $745 stop above, set the payday target to $2,300. The blended plan leaves $53.66 more of the driver's SOL available today and $49.12 less cash on payday than borrowing the full $95. Pure sale misses the payday target by $52.56. These amounts compare modeled plans.

**Show a loan FreightOS would turn down.** Restore the $190 sample. Change the bill to $186. With accounts ready, borrowing 57¢ leaves $10.95 on payday at $150/SOL. Check **Include first-time loan setup**. Selling enough SOL for 57¢ now leaves $10.38. Open **What does my first loan need?** to see why the best new loan leaves $9.26. With this wallet and these customer terms, selling also wins at every nonnegative payday SOL price. The comparison assumes the same recorded terms and freight payment in full.

**Keep more cash for the road.** Open `/fuel-desk?example=balanced-route&view=pledge`. “I have $30 for the rest of this trip. **Sell or pledge?** Open `/fuel-desk?example=balanced-route&view=sale`. “I want the same $22.24 in my pocket. **Price a bigger fall before payday.** Open `/fuel-desk?example=balanced-route&view=downside`. “I have $30. Switch to 45% and recheck the newly selected amount on public Devnet.

**Protect the pledge before payday.** Open `/fuel-desk?example=balanced-route&view=midtrip`. “I have $30 halfway through my trip. ### Before presenting

1. Start the packaged app on the presentation laptop. Use the normal local setup in the [README](README.md). Keep the laptop awake and open the Overview, paid receipt at `/public-offer`, timing switch at `/public-offer#fuel-cover`, and settled loan at `/load#credit` in separate tabs.
2. Wait for the receipt breakdown and recorded fuel comparison to load. Restore **Before I can spend a payout**. Confirm the projector shows both columns and test the venue network once with **Verify on public Solana**.
3. If verification stalls on stage, use the saved receipt and say “This is the saved finalized transaction.” If the app or projector connection fails, play the captioned MP4 from the laptop. At a 30-second cutoff, open `/load#credit`, point out the vendor payment and the returned pledge, and open either Explorer receipt.

### Questions worth rehearsing

- **Where is the edge?** The engine checks dated cash flows across six Gemini fuel budgets and compares contract terms against spendable reserve proceeds. In the 24-cent plan, 23 cents buys the contract including fees and one cent stays for diesel. If the payout is late at $93 oil, the reserve preview checks the missing $10.50. The payment approval binds the chosen terms and vendor.
- **How real is it?** Both parties used sponsored Devnet funds. The settlement is signed. The $4.96 difference is a modeled fee comparison for this load. The next-trip example prices a hypothetical hedge from recorded offers. No Gemini order was placed.
- **Who repaid the loan?** The demo borrower used sponsored test USDC acquired through Orca. Repayment followed three seconds after borrowing. A 23-day cost is a projection. A driver needs collateral, and a sufficiently large price drop can trigger liquidation. The two network fees totaled 0.000010 test SOL. The borrower profile retained 0.0058928 test SOL of account rent.
- **What if the customer allows a smaller advance?** Open `/fuel-credit#partial-advance`. On the same late-payday $3 offer, the driver asks for $173.33 of the $200 invoice. The customer sends $170.73 after the fee. The vault supplies $19.27. Compare the $2.66 modeled cost with $3 for full early pay and $3.19 for the loan. - **Would FreightOS ever tell the driver to skip the loan?** At `/fuel-credit#one-payment`, enter a $3 early-pay fee. On the ordinary 23-day case the combined loan costs $1.24.
